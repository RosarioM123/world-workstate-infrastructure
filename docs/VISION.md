# WORLD: The Vision in One Page

## The one secret

Intelligence is becoming stateless. Every time a person or an
organization switches AI models or tools, the accumulated context,
decisions, and hard-won judgment are lost and rebuilt from zero.
Models get cheaper and more interchangeable every quarter; what does
not get cheaper is the institutional memory of what was tried, what
was decided, and why.

WORLD's bet: the durable value sits in a persistent, verifiable layer
of state *above* the models, not in any single model. Treat the model
as a replaceable worker. The accumulated project memory is the asset
that compounds.

## The three layers

- **WORLD: the persistent intelligence layer.** A durable, shared,
  verifiable record of work. Agents submit intents, a constraint engine
  returns COMMITTED or REJECTED, and every attempt, legal or rogue, is
  appended to a hash-chained ledger. This repository is WORLD v0: the
  state kernel.
- **MIND: the collective AI workforce.** Specialized agents (research,
  coding, financial, and more) operating on top of WORLD's shared
  state, delegating to each other and resuming each other's work with
  full context. Built second, because a workforce with no shared
  institutional memory is just a demo that forgets.
- **REALITY: the action layer.** The interface between digital
  intelligence and the physical and external world: APIs, robots,
  laboratories, factories, vehicles, financial systems. Built last,
  because it is the highest-stakes layer and needs the safeguards and
  shared state of the first two underneath it.

## The build order is the discipline

Brain first, workforce second, hands last. Each layer is only built
once the one beneath it is real and validated with evidence, never on
the promise that the foundation will arrive later.

## What would prove it wrong

The thesis dies if any of these turn out true: teams do not feel the
pain of lost context strongly enough to adopt shared state; the
switching costs of embedding in model selection, budgets, and policy
never materialize; or verifiable state turns out to be a feature of
existing databases rather than a layer of its own. The experiments in
`docs/` exist to test exactly these claims.
