## 1. Updated thesis

Disney enters the 3-5 year horizon as a company whose streaming turnaround is real and now banked, whose parks monetize pricing power more than volume, whose sports business is mid-transition with undisclosed net economics, whose leadership is stable but strategically unproven under its new CEO, and whose competitive environment is hostage to an unresolved asset contest.

**Streaming is the core profit engine.** The Direct-to-Consumer segment flipped from roughly $4B in annual losses in FY2022 to sustained profitability: Q2 FY2026 operating income of $582M (+88%, the first quarter above 10% margin), then $712M in Q3 FY2026 at a 13% margin, on subscription streaming revenue of $5.53B (+11%) and ad revenue of $851M (+3%). Management's full-year FY2026 target is at least 10% DTC operating margin. D2 stands as the primary driver, with one refinement: the easy hundred-point wins of the turnaround are banked, and Phase 2 should model a maturing margin curve (converging toward the mid-teens) rather than extrapolating the 2023-2026 recovery slope. The handoff contains no cost-side data for margins materially above the mid-teens, so anything past that is speculative.

**Parks and Experiences is a pricing-power business with a capacity-expansion kicker.** Domestic attendance was flat to down (Q2 FY2026 -1% with Experiences operating income still +5%) while per-capita spending rose 4-5% and ticket revenue per guest rose 5%. Q3 FY2026 showed Experiences revenue near $10B with operating income up 20% to $3.02B and domestic attendance +3%, but the consistent pattern is income growth led by pricing and per-cap spend, not turnstile counts. Cruise capacity expanded 50% with new ships, a volume leg distinct from parks attendance. The unhedged risk is the pricing ceiling: the handoff has no satisfaction or elasticity data, so the risk is flagged, not quantified (new Q6).

**Sports is a two-act structure with disclosed economics only on the first act.** Fiscal 2025 sports operating income was $2.88B (+20%), and in fiscal Q4 2025 the DTC component ($352M OI, +39%) nearly matched the linear networks component ($391M, -21%). ESPN's direct-to-consumer product launched August 21, 2025 at $29.99/month; Disney acquired NFL Network and RedZone in a deal giving the NFL a 10% equity stake in ESPN, with WWE premium live events exclusive to ESPN from 2026. The DTC business exists and grows, but nothing in the handoff proves it is net accretive once affiliate-fee cannibalization is counted. This is the highest-uncertainty segment and where Phase 2 discipline (G1, verify from the 10-K/10-Q) matters most.

**Succession is resolved; strategy is inherited, not yet re-authored.** Josh D'Amaro was named CEO effective March 18, 2026 by unanimous board vote, with Dana Walden as President and Chief Creative Officer and Iger remaining senior advisor through December 31, 2026. His May 2026 earnings debut was well received (+7-8% stock pop). But Phase 1's search for D'Amaro-era pillars distinct from Iger's four priorities found nothing: through September 2026, communications still quote Iger's framework. Succession reduces execution risk; D'Amaro's strategic authorship is the key qualitative unknown, and the handoff provides no evidence either way.

**The WBD contest is a competitive variable, not yet a Disney P&L line item.** The December 2025 Netflix agreement to buy WBD's studio/streaming unit ($82.7B, $27.75/share) conflicts with the February 2026 report that Netflix declined to raise its bid and the WBD board found Paramount Skydance's $31/share proposal superior. Per G3, this continuation does not average or pick; it carries the conflict. The thesis-level point: the eventual owner materially changes Disney's competitive geometry. Phase 2 must be scenario-based here.

**Franchise reliability is constrained, but the slate is not.** D10 stands, strengthened by the evidence: The Mandalorian and Grogu at $345.2M is the lowest-grossing live-action Star Wars film; live-action Moana at roughly $263M gross against roughly $250M budget is at best a wash. Yet the studio system works: Toy Story 5 crossed $1B (third Toy Story film to do so), and Disney was the first studio to $3B global in 2026. The refinement: distinguish franchise-line reliability (constrained) from studio-system reliability (demonstrably functioning). An elaboration of D10, not a reversal.

**Capital allocation supports the downside case.** The dividend rose 50% to $1.50/share and the buyback to at least $9B for FY2026 (from $7B), funded by roughly $10B of free cash flow ($19B operating cash flow guidance minus $9B capex). This is downside support through return of capital, not a growth driver. Execution risk is real: a $9B program's average execution price determines the per-share value created, and the handoff has no execution detail (Q4).

**The Epic Games stake remains option value.** The $1.5B stake (February 2024), with a persistent Disney universe interoperating with Fortnite, has produced no launch date and no metrics through September 2026. It stays out of the base case (D7); on this continuation's judgment it is a late-horizon event at best.

**Financial health is verify-before-trust.** Beyond FY2025 trailing figures (revenue $94.4B, net income $12.0B, adjusted EPS $5.93 up 19%) and guidance (double-digit adjusted EPS growth FY26/FY27, $19B operating cash flow), this continuation takes no position on the balance sheet. G1 requires primary-filing verification; the A9 staleness flag stands.
