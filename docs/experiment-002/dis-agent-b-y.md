## 1. Updated thesis

Disney is a mid-transformation conglomerate whose investment story now hinges on execution rather than rescue. The legacy narrative of streaming losses being subsidized by parks is stale. Disney+ and Hulu generate real double-digit operating margins, ESPN's direct-to-consumer app is live, and the parks segment is compounding on pricing and capacity rather than raw attendance volume. Three genuinely open questions will determine whether the equity re-rates over the 3-5 year horizon: (a) whether streaming margins can expand from the low teens toward the high teens; (b) whether the D'Amaro/Walden leadership team can run the franchise flywheel at the hit rate of the 2015-2019 peak, given visible misses in Star Wars and live-action remakes; and (c) whether the linear networks decline stabilizes into a managed glide path rather than an accelerating decay that offsets DTC gains.

My working thesis frames earnings power as three engines in sequence. Streaming profitability expansion is the near-term engine, already evident with management guiding at least 10% margins and Q3 FY2026 at 13%. Parks and Experiences remain the durable cash engine, with cruise capacity as the most legible growth lever and per-capita spending as the pricing lever. ESPN's DTC transition is the wildcard engine, with the NFL equity stake and WWE exclusivity providing content moats but with net cannibalization effects still unproven. The risk side is concentrated in franchise inconsistency and the unresolved competitive landscape around Warner Bros. Discovery assets, where conflicting claims remain genuinely unresolved and must not be treated as settled fact.

I attach no valuation and no buy/hold/sell recommendation; that belongs to a later phase. The thesis is an earnings-power framing: the business mix is healthier than the 2023-2024 market narrative suggested, but the next 3-5 years depend on management executing a studio slate at a hit rate the last two years have not reliably shown.

## 3. Assumptions revisited

**A1: "Streaming can reach 15-20% margins." Status: REVISED. Confidence: moderate that 10-13% sustains; low that 15-20% arrives within the window.** Q2 at 10.6% and Q3 at 13%, with full-year guidance of at least 10% versus about 5% in FY2025, is a strong run. But the leap to high-teens margins has no disclosed driver yet. Bundling economics post-Hulu buyout and ESPN DTC contribution are candidate drivers, but neither is quantified in the handoff. This is the single most important assumption for the earnings thesis, and it needs Phase 3 modeling, not assertion.

**A2: "Parks growth is durable at roughly 30% margins." Status: CONFIRMED as directionally sound, with a scope qualification.** Experiences operating income grew 20% on 10% revenue growth in Q3 FY2026, with roughly 30% margins through nine months. Domestic attendance up 3%, per-cap spend up, ticket revenue per guest up 5%, and cruise stateroom capacity up about 50% year over year. That is a multi-driver story, not a single point of failure. The qualification: roughly 30% is an excellent margin and likely near ceiling; growth must come from volume (cruise staterooms, Abu Dhabi over time) and pricing rather than margin expansion. Confidence: high on segment durability, moderate on sustaining 20% operating income growth rates specifically.

**A3: "The franchise flywheel still works at the old hit rate." Status: downgraded.** Toy Story 5 crossed $1B and Disney hit $3B global box office first in 2026, which is genuinely strong. But The Mandalorian and Grogu at $345.2M is the lowest-grossing live-action Star Wars to date, and live-action Moana grossed roughly $263M against roughly $250M production budget, approximately breakeven before marketing. D'Amaro's framing that franchise value extends beyond theaters into retail, parks attractions, gaming, and Disney+ is a real mitigating argument, and I take it seriously. But theatrical misses are the leading indicator of franchise health, and two tentpole-tier misses in one year is a pattern worth tracking, not dismissing. Confidence: low to moderate that the historical hit rate is intact. This is the principal downside risk to the thesis.

**A4: "Linear networks are a managed decline, not a cliff." Status: REVISED, with a warning flag.** DTC operating income nearly matched linear networks' profit in fiscal Q4 2025 ($352M vs $391M, linear down 21% year over year). That is a steep decay rate, and the crossing point where DTC fully replaces linear profit may already have arrived. That is good news for the business mix narrative but it also means the decline is faster than "managed glide path" suggests. Confidence: moderate that the decline is manageable given DTC's growth, low that it is a gentle glide. The right frame: linear is shrinking fast and DTC must keep growing fast enough to more than offset it.

**A5: "Balance sheet supports the buyback and dividend program." Status: CONFIRMED with a verification caveat.** FY2025 free cash flow around $10.08B, operating cash flow $18.1B, FY2026 guidance of $19B operating cash flow against roughly $9B capex, a $1.50 dividend (+50%), and buybacks rising to at least $9B in FY2026: the cash flows arithmetically cover the shareholder return program. The net debt/EBITDA figure of roughly 1.9x comes from a single third-party note and the journal itself flags it as needing verification from filings. Confidence: high on the cash flow arithmetic; moderate on the leverage ratio until verified.

**A6: "ESPN DTC cannibalization is a manageable, unproven risk." Status: carried forward unchanged; evidence base thin.** The flagship app launched August 21, 2025 at $29.99/month with a launch bundle at the same price for 12 months; NFL Network and RedZone are coming to ESPN; the NFL took a 10% equity stake; WWE premium live events are exclusive from 2026. Management called the launch a success, but whether DTC subscribers are cord-nevers versus cord-cutters migrating at lower economics is explicitly unproven. I formalize this as an open question with a defined test (sports segment operating income trend, Q3 FY2026 guided down 14% on rights timing) and move it into next steps.

## 6. New decisions

**D1: Adopt a three-engine sequencing frame for the 3-5 year earnings story.** The handoff's evidence decomposes naturally into streaming profitability (near-term, already inflecting), parks and cruise (durable base, compounding on pricing and capacity), and ESPN DTC (wildcard with content moats but unproven cannibalization). Sequencing by timing and certainty gives the Phase 3 model a structure: near-term earnings growth is streaming-led, mid-window growth is parks-led, and the ESPN outcome determines whether the outer years surprise to the upside or disappoint.

**D2: Establish a franchise hit-rate monitoring program as a formal downside indicator.** The 2026 slate's unevenness is the largest source of variance in the thesis. The monitoring rule: track each fiscal year's tentpole slate on theatrical return on production budget and franchise extension value (parks, retail, Disney+, gaming). A second consecutive year with multiple tentpole theatrical misses, without offsetting extension value, would force a downgrade of the content-moat assumption. D'Amaro's "value beyond theaters" framing is accepted as a legitimate offset, but it must be evidenced per franchise, not asserted in aggregate.

**D3: Treat linear networks as a fast decline requiring fast DTC offset, not a gentle glide.** Linear down 21% year over year with DTC nearly matching its profit is a steep decay. The implication for Phase 3: stress-test a scenario where linear profits fall faster than the base case while DTC margins expand only at the guided rather than the aspirational rate. The margin of safety in the earnings thesis is thinner than the "managed decline" phrasing implies.

**D4: Model the Abu Dhabi resort as royalty option value, not base-case earnings.** The Miral structure (no Disney capital, no ownership, royalty income) is attractive on a return-on-capital basis, but with no disclosed opening timeline or royalty terms, putting it in the base case would be fabrication. Option value is the honest treatment; disclosed milestones move it into the case.

**D5: Make ESPN DTC net economics the highest-priority Phase 3 evidence target.** Among the open questions, this one has the largest earnings swing potential and the thinnest evidence base. The content moats (NFL equity stake, RedZone, WWE exclusivity) are real assets, but the cannibalization question is binary for the sports segment's profit trajectory. The next research block should go to sports segment disclosures and affiliate fee trends before anything else.

**D6: Keep the shareholder return program as a signal of management confidence, not as thesis evidence.** The dividend increase (+50% to $1.50) and the buyback escalation (to at least $9B in FY2026) are well covered by guided cash flows, and they signal that D'Amaro's team believes the earnings trajectory. But buybacks and dividends distribute earnings power; they do not create it. I note them as a confidence signal and a floor under per-share metrics, not as a driver.
