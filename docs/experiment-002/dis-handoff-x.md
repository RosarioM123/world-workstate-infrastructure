## What the research started from, and what changed

The first researcher pre-registered three naive priors before touching any data, and the evidence forced changes to all three.

First, the old belief that streaming is a drag on earnings is dead. Streaming crossed into sustained profitability: in fiscal Q2 2026 (quarter ended March 28) the streaming business generated $5.49B in revenue (+13%) and $582M in operating income (+88% year over year), its first quarter above a double-digit operating margin (10.6%). Fiscal Q3 2026 went further, with streaming operating income more than doubling to $712M at a 13% margin. Management has reaffirmed a full-year target of at least 10% streaming operating margin, up from about 5% in FY2025. Compare this to fiscal 2022, when streaming lost roughly $4B for the year. The framing that now fits the evidence: streaming is a profit engine, not the burn-phase business it was three years ago. Parks and Experiences remain the largest earner, but the profit story for the next 3-5 years is the streaming margin inflection.

Second, the belief that Universal's Epic Universe would take meaningful attendance share from Disney World was revised downward. Epic Universe is real (opened mid-2025, roughly a $7B investment by Comcast/Universal), and MoffettNathanson had projected roughly a million visitor impact on Disney World in 2025-26. But Disney's domestic park attendance held: it was up 3% in fiscal Q3 2026 (recovering from a weak Q2 where it was down 1%), and Disney's CFO described Epic Universe's impact on Disney attendance as minimal. September 2026 reporting even says Universal itself is seeing softer attendance while Disney's "exceeded expectations." The honest revision: the threat is not hype, but it has not materialized as share loss for Disney so far. The domestic parks growth story is pricing-led, not volume-led (more on this below).

Third, the assumption that Bob Iger is still CEO with succession unresolved was flatly reversed. Josh D'Amaro, a 28-year Disney veteran and former chair of Disney Experiences, was named CEO effective March 18, 2026 (announced February 3, 2026). Iger serves as senior advisor and board member until his retirement on December 31, 2026. Dana Walden became President and Chief Creative Officer. Board chair James Gorman (ex-Morgan Stanley) led the succession process. D'Amaro's first earnings print on May 6, 2026 beat consensus by about $320M and the stock popped 7-8%. He has publicly called Disney+ the company's "digital centerpiece."

## The current state of each business segment

**Streaming.** See the profit inflection above. Key sources: the ainvest piece from August 2026, the momoview recap of Q2 FY2026, and thedesk.net's Q3 FY2026 report. One hard constraint for your work: Disney has stopped disclosing per-service subscriber counts (the last verifiable combined figure the first researcher found was 195.7-196M in fiscal Q4 2025), so you cannot verify subscriber growth directly. Work from revenue, operating income, and ARPU lines only; do not impute per-service subscriber counts.

**Parks and Experiences.** This is the profit engine: fiscal Q3 2026 revenue was about $9.97B (+10%), operating income $3.02B (+20%), with roughly a 30% segment operating margin through the first nine months of FY2026. Domestic park attendance rose 3% in fiscal Q3 while per-cap spend rose about 4-5% and average ticket revenue per guest was up 5%. The Disney Cruise Line added roughly 50% stateroom capacity year over year with the Disney Destiny and Disney Adventure. International visitation to US parks has been soft but is improving. A 10-year, $60B Experiences capex plan (new lands, cruise ships, tech) is underway; management says the projects should deliver double-digit lifetime returns. The central modeling decision to inherit: treat domestic parks growth as pricing and per-cap-spend led, with attendance flat to modest. Three quarters of data showed operating income growing on flat-to-down attendance (Q2 FY2026: attendance -1%, operating income +5%), and an earlier working decision to model attendance-led volume growth was explicitly superseded by the evidence.

**Linear networks and sports.** Linear networks keep declining: in fiscal Q4 2025 their operating income was $391M, down 21% year over year, and streaming operating income ($352M, +39%) nearly matched it. Sports segment operating income was $2.88B in FY2025 (+20%), though fiscal Q3 2026 was guided down about 14% year over year on rights-deal timing (NFL and new agreements). The big strategic move: the ESPN direct-to-consumer flagship app launched August 21, 2025 at $29.99/month inside the ESPN App, covering all ESPN linear networks plus ESPN+, with a launch bundle of Disney+/Hulu at $29.99/month for the first 12 months. Related moves: ESPN is acquiring NFL Network and RedZone while the NFL takes a 10% equity stake in ESPN (announced August 5, 2025), and WWE premium live events (including WrestleMania) became exclusive to ESPN platforms starting in 2026. The inherited stance: treat ESPN DTC as a strategic option with unproven net economics. Management calls it "off to a success," but the net cannibalization of pay-TV affiliate fees is unquantified, so do not assume it offsets linear decline one-for-one.

**Studio slate.** Uneven but headline-strong. Toy Story 5 crossed $1B worldwide (the third film of 2026 to do so, with a $160M domestic / $312M worldwide opening), and Disney was the first studio to $3B global box office in 2026. But two misses were acknowledged by D'Amaro himself: The Mandalorian and Grogu ($345.2M worldwide, the lowest-grossing live-action Star Wars film to date) and the live-action Moana (about $263M gross against a roughly $250M production budget, before marketing costs). D'Amaro's stated framing is that franchise investments create value beyond theaters, through retail, parks attractions, gaming, and Disney+. Devil Wears Prada 2 did $677-691M; Pixar's original Hoppers did $372-383M; calendar 2025 had Zootopia 2 and Avatar: Fire and Ash as billion-dollar hits. The inherited constraint: do not assume Star Wars or live-action remakes are automatic theatrical hits in any model you build. Franchise reliability is uneven; animated tentpoles still deliver, but the "content machine" assumption needs qualification.

**Financial health.** FY2025: revenue $94.4B (+3%), net income $12.0B (up from $7.6B), adjusted EPS $5.93 (+19%), free cash flow about $10.08B, operating cash flow $18.1B. FY2026 guidance: $19B operating cash flow, about $9B capex, and double-digit adjusted EPS growth in both FY26 and FY27. The dividend is now $1.50/share for 2026 (+50% year over year, continuing the post-COVID reinstatement cadence). The buyback target was raised through the year from $7B to $8B to at least $9B in FY2026. Treat the dividend plus buyback as shareholder-return discipline that supports the downside, not as a growth driver; these programs reflect maturity, not expansion. One caution: the balance sheet picture (net debt/EBITDA about 1.9x, lowest since 2018; debt paid down from $54.4B to $42.0B over five years) comes from a single third-party research note and was NOT verified from filings. Verify it from the 10-K before you model.

**Leadership.** Succession is resolved: D'Amaro CEO, Walden President and CCO, Iger senior advisor through December 31, 2026.

**M&A and partnerships.** Three items. First, the Hulu buyout is complete: Disney paid Comcast about $9.05B total ($8.61B in December 2023 plus $438.7M after a third-party appraisal completed June 9, 2025) for the final 33%, enabling full Hulu/Disney+/ESPN integration. Second, Abu Dhabi: a seventh Disney resort was announced May 7, 2025 with Miral on Yas Island. It is fully funded, built, and operated by Miral; Disney provides creative design and oversight and earns royalties. There is no Disney capital and no ownership stake. Inherited modeling decision: exclude Abu Dhabi from capex entirely and model only royalty revenue. Third, gaming: a $1.5B equity stake in Epic Games (announced February 2024) to build a persistent Disney universe interoperating with Fortnite; it is a multiyear project with no launch date disclosed. Inherited stance: carry the Epic Games stake as option value, not as a thesis pillar. Also noted: Hulu + Live TV combined with Fubo (closed 2025), visible in Q3 FY2026 subscription and affiliate revenue.

## The one genuine conflict: do not resolve it yourself

One set of directly conflicting claims was deliberately preserved as a conflict, and you must do the same. Netflix agreed in December 2025 to acquire Warner Bros. Discovery's studio and streaming assets for about $82.7B ($27.75/share), but a February 2026 report says Netflix declined to raise its bid and Paramount Skydance ($31/share) won WBD's board recommendation. The sources disagree on the current status. Do not average the two claims and do not silently pick one. Carry the WBD deal as an unresolved competitive overhang with no point forecast, disclosed as a conflict. Finding its actual status is one of your open questions, and the combined entity's meaning for Disney's streaming competition is a real 3-5 year variable.
