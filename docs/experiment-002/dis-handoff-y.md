## Entry 2026-09-22 (start of day): Pre-registered naive priors

These are my beliefs BEFORE any research today. They will be revisited and marked survived / revised / reversed.

- **P1 (prior):** Streaming is still mostly a drag on Disney's earnings. Parks and Experiences carry the company almost alone.
- **P2 (prior):** Disney's Orlando parks dominance is unassailable; Universal's Epic Universe is hype, not a real attendance threat.
- **P3 (prior):** Bob Iger is still CEO and succession is unresolved; there is no clear next CEO.

## Entry 2026-09-22 (research block 1): Streaming economics

- Researched Disney+ / Hulu / ESPN DTC profitability. Found: fiscal Q2 2026 streaming revenue $5.49B, +13%, operating income $582M, +88% YoY, first quarter above double-digit operating margin (10.6%). Management reaffirmed full-year target of at least 10% streaming operating margin, up from about 5% in FY2025.
- Sources: ainvest piece (Aug 2026), momoview Q2 FY2026 recap, thedesk.net Q3 FY2026 report (streaming OI more than doubled to $712M in fiscal Q3, 13% margin).
- Verdict on P1: **REVISED**. Streaming crossed into sustained profitability; it is now a profit engine, not the burn-phase drag of FY2022 (~$4B annual losses). Parks remain the biggest earner, but the "streaming is the drag" framing is outdated.

## Entry 2026-09-22 (research block 2): Parks and Experiences

- Experiences segment: FY Q3 2026 revenue ~$9.97B (+10%), operating income $3.02B (+20%); ~30% operating margin through first nine months of FY2026. Domestic park attendance up 3% in fiscal Q3; per-cap spend up; average ticket revenue per guest up 5%.
- Cruise line: Disney Destiny and Disney Adventure added ~50% stateroom capacity vs prior-year quarter; a driver of growth.
- Universal Epic Universe (opened mid-2025): MoffettNathanson projected ~1M visitor impact on Disney World in 2025-26; Disney CFO Hugh Johnston said Epic Universe's impact on Disney attendance was minimal. September 2026 reporting says Universal itself is seeing softer attendance, and Disney's attendance "exceeded expectations."
- Verdict on P2: **REVISED**. Epic Universe is real and Universal invested ~$7B, but Disney's attendance is holding (flat to slightly up) and growth comes from per-cap spend, not volume. The threat is not hype, but it has not yet materialized as a share loss for Disney.

## Entry 2026-09-22 (research block 3): Linear networks, sports rights, ESPN DTC

- ESPN direct-to-consumer flagship app launched August 21, 2025 at $29.99/month, inside the ESPN App, covering all ESPN linear networks plus ESPN+. Bundled with Disney+/Hulu at launch for $29.99/month for the first 12 months.
- Related sports moves: ESPN acquiring NFL Network and RedZone; NFL taking a 10% equity stake in ESPN (announced Aug 5, 2025). WWE premium live events (incl. WrestleMania) became exclusive to ESPN platforms starting 2026.
- Sports segment: FY2025 operating income $2.88B, up 20%; Q3 FY2026 guided sports OI down ~14% YoY on rights-deal timing.
- Linear networks keep declining: DTC OI nearly matched linear networks' profit in fiscal Q4 2025 ($352M vs $391M, linear down 21% YoY).
- ESPN DTC success called out by management as "off to a success"; net cannibalization effect still unproven.

## Entry 2026-09-22 (research block 4): Studio slate

- Toy Story 5 crossed $1B worldwide (third film of 2026 to do so), opening $160M domestic / $312M worldwide. Disney hit $3B global box office before any other studio in 2026.
- Misses acknowledged by CEO D'Amaro: The Mandalorian and Grogu ($345.2M worldwide, lowest-grossing live-action Star Wars to date) and live-action Moana (~$263M gross vs ~$250M production budget). D'Amaro's framing: franchise investments create value beyond theaters (retail, parks attractions, gaming, Disney+).
- Devil Wears Prada 2 hit ~$677-691M. Pixar's original Hoppers ~$372-383M.
- Finding: franchise reliability is uneven; animated tentpoles still deliver, but live-action remakes and Star Wars are not automatic hits. This qualifies any "content machine" assumption.

## Entry 2026-09-22 (research block 5): Financial health

- FY2025: revenue $94.4B (+3%), net income $12.0B (from $7.6B), adjusted EPS $5.93 (+19%), free cash flow ~$10.08B, operating cash flow $18.1B.
- FY2026 guidance: $19B operating cash flow, ~$9B capex, double-digit adjusted EPS growth in FY26 and FY27.
- Shareholder returns: dividend reinstated post-COVID, now $1.50/share for 2026 (+50% from prior year). Buyback target raised through the year: $7B, then $8B, then at least $9B in FY2026 (per fiscal Q3 report).
- Balance sheet (third-party research note, single source, lower confidence): net debt/EBITDA ~1.9x, lowest since 2018, debt paydown from $54.4B to $42.0B over five years. Note: verify from filings before modeling.

## Entry 2026-09-22 (research block 6): Leadership succession

- Verdict on P3: **REVERSED**. Josh D'Amaro (28-year Disney veteran, ex-chair of Disney Experiences) was named CEO effective March 18, 2026, announced Feb 3, 2026. Bob Iger serves as senior advisor and board member until his retirement Dec 31, 2026. Dana Walden became President and Chief Creative Officer. Board chair James Gorman (ex-Morgan Stanley) led the process.
- First earnings print under D'Amaro (May 6, 2026) beat consensus by ~$320M; stock popped 7-8%. He called Disney+ the company's "digital centerpiece."

## Entry 2026-09-22 (research block 7): M&A, partnerships, competitive position

- Hulu buyout complete: Disney paid Comcast ~$9.05B total ($8.61B in Dec 2023 + $438.7M after third-party appraisal in June 2025) for the final 33%. Enables full Hulu/Disney+/ESPN integration.
- Abu Dhabi: seventh Disney resort announced May 7, 2025 with Miral on Yas Island. Fully funded, built, and operated by Miral; Disney provides creative design and oversight and earns royalties. No Disney capital, no ownership stake.
- Gaming: $1.5B equity stake in Epic Games announced Feb 2024; building a persistent Disney universe connected to Fortnite. Multiyear project, no launch date disclosed.
- Disney-Fubo: Hulu + Live TV combined with Fubo (referenced in Q3 FY2026 results via subscription/affiliate revenue line).
- Competitive conflict found: Netflix agreed to buy WBD's studio/streaming assets for ~$82.7B ($27.75/share, Dec 2025), but a Feb 2026 report says Netflix declined to raise its bid and Paramount Skydance ($31/share, $30 then $31) won WBD's board recommendation. Sources conflict on current status; flagged as unresolved, not modeled as a fact.
