## 1. Updated Investment Thesis (3–5 Year Horizon, Intel Corporation)

Intel is a process-turnaround and sovereign-capacity story whose investment case rests on two independent validation gates, not one: (a) internal 18A execution — yield and cost trajectory through the Panther Lake ramp and Clearwater Forest/Xeon 6+ on 18A in H1 2026 — and (b) external foundry commercialization, defined as at least one significant 14A customer commitment. Gate (a) is partially in evidence and directionally positive; gate (b) has no evidence at all: only $293 million of Q2 2026's $5.8 billion in foundry revenue was external, and only $307 million of FY2025's $17.8 billion.

The near-term financial picture is optically bad but structurally improving. Q2 2026 GAAP net income of $(11.0) billion is dominated by a $12.5 billion non-cash mark-to-market on escrowed shares and is non-informative; non-GAAP EPS of $0.42 and a foundry operating-margin improvement from −71.7% to −36.2% on internal volume are the operative signals. DCAI grew 59% to $6.3 billion and is reportedly supply-constrained — a genuine demand signal.

The bear case is structural, not cyclical: AMD holds 46.2% of server CPU revenue share (Q1 2026) versus ~34% unit share — AMD is winning the high-value mix while Intel keeps a unit majority. Intel has no merchant GPU exposure (Falcon Shores cancelled as a product, pivot to Jaguar Shores/rack-scale, Gaudi disappointing), so AI exposure must be evaluated through Xeon, AI PCs, ASICs, packaging, and NVIDIA-linked products. The balance sheet is leveraged (LT debt $48.5B, ST $2.0B against ~$30B cash + marketable securities, ~$20.8B net debt — secondary sources) and the foundry buildout is dilution-funded: government 433.3M shares (9.9%), NVIDIA 214.7M shares ($5B), a reported (unverified) $20B August 2026 raise, SoftBank $2B. The cumulative dilution tally is the single most important unquantified item in the case.

The government stake is both strategic asset and structural constraint: $5.7B early CHIPS cash and a too-important-to-fail position, against 9.9% dilution, a 5-year $20 warrant for an extra 5% if Intel holds under 51% of its foundry, and guardrails that likely bar foundry separation through the window. NVIDIA is both the dominant AI competitor and a $5B investor/partner (closed December 2025), with joint NVLink products, custom x86, and RTX PC SoCs as optionality — not revenue. The dividend is zero and stays zero in the base case.

Net thesis: Intel's three-to-five-year outcome is binary-on-two-gates. If 18A cost trajectory holds AND a significant external 14A customer materializes, the foundry is a real second business. If either gate fails — particularly the external one, where there is zero evidence today — Intel remains a PC/server CPU company in structural revenue-share decline, carrying a leveraged, dilution-funded balance sheet with no dividend. I keep the inherited frame: the external-customer test — not the 18A ramp alone — is the dispositive evidence for the foundry thesis.
