## 1. Updated thesis

The investment case for Disney over the next 3 to 5 years is a margin story, not a volume story. The Phase 1 evidence supports keeping the inherited framing, but it needs sharpening around what kind of margin story this is and where it can break.

Streaming is no longer the question; it is the answer's first leg. The business moved from roughly $4B in annual streaming losses in fiscal 2022 to $582M of operating income at a 10.6% margin in fiscal Q2 2026, then $712M at 13% in fiscal Q3 2026, against a full-year target of at least 10%, up from about 5% in FY2025. The thesis update is that the operative question has shifted from "can streaming make money" to "how high can the combined margin go, and how durable is the pricing power behind it."

Parks and Experiences remain the profit engine, and they are the second leg, but the mechanism matters. Fiscal Q3 2026 showed revenue of about $9.97B (+10%) and operating income of $3.02B (+20%), with roughly a 30% segment operating margin through the first nine months of FY2026. The inherited modeling decision holds: domestic parks growth is pricing-led and per-cap-spend-led, with attendance flat to modest, as shown by fiscal Q2 2026 where attendance fell 1% while operating income rose 5%. This means both profit legs of the thesis are pricing and ARPU levers, which is higher-quality earnings than volume chasing, but it concentrates the risk in consumer elasticity and pricing power. There is no broad volume engine in the base case.

The exception that proves the point is the Disney Cruise Line, which added roughly 50% stateroom capacity year over year with the Disney Destiny and Disney Adventure. That is genuine capacity-led volume growth, and it deserves to be modeled separately from the pricing-led domestic parks rather than folded into one Experiences number. It also interacts with the 10-year, $60B Experiences capex plan, whose promised double-digit lifetime returns must be earned project by project, not assumed.

Linear networks are the acknowledged drag, declining at a double-digit pace (operating income down 21% year over year in fiscal Q4 2025 to $391M), and the ESPN direct-to-consumer flagship app, launched August 21, 2025 at $29.99/month, is a strategic option rather than a proven offset. The thesis treats ESPN DTC as asymmetric optionality on the sports rights portfolio, strengthened by the ESPN-NFL Network/RedZone deal and the NFL's 10% equity stake in ESPN.

The studio slate is a variance contributor with capped upside, not a growth engine. Toy Story 5 crossed $1B worldwide and Disney was the first studio to $3B global box office in 2026, but The Mandalorian and Grogu at $345.2M worldwide (the lowest-grossing live-action Star Wars film) and the live-action Moana at roughly $263M gross against a roughly $250M production budget show franchise reliability is uneven. Animated tentpoles remain the reliable engine; everything else is optionality.

The hinge variable for the whole thesis is the FY2026 streaming margin target of at least 10% and whether margins compound past it or plateau there. If streaming margins hold above 10% while parks pricing power persists and linear declines at the historical pace, the thesis earns a positive directional call. If streaming margin expansion stalls at the 10% target and parks pricing hits elasticity limits simultaneously, the thesis breaks, because there is no third leg.

## 2. Inherited decisions: keep / change

The inherited decision was correct for the parks that existed; the fleet expansion changed the composition.

**Keep: ESPN DTC treated as a strategic option with unproven net economics.** The base case must not assume it offsets linear decline one-for-one.

**Keep: no automatic theatrical hits assumed for Star Wars or live-action remakes.** The Mandalorian and Grogu and Moana data points are decisive enough that this guardrail should be permanent, not provisional.

**Keep: Abu Dhabi excluded from capex, royalty-only modeling.** High-confidence and sourced to Disney's own announcements.

**Keep: the Epic Games stake carried as option value.** A $1.5B equity position announced February 2024 with no launch date and no progress updates cannot support a thesis pillar. Option value is the correct accounting.

**Keep: dividend ($1.50/share for 2026, +50%) and the raised buyback target (at least $9B for FY2026) as downside support, not a growth driver.**

**Keep: FY2026 management guidance (double-digit adjusted EPS growth in FY26 and FY27, $19B operating cash flow) as the public-anchor baseline to be tested, not as fact.** The model must be able to disagree with guidance.

**Keep: succession scored as reduced execution risk versus an external hire, with Iger's presence through December 31, 2026 flagged as residual transition risk.** The May 6, 2026 beat and the 7-8% stock pop support the reduced-risk score; the absence of CEO-specific strategy supports the residual flag.

**Keep: the WBD acquisition status carried as a disclosed conflict, never averaged or silently resolved.**

**Keep: no per-service subscriber count imputation; revenue, operating income, and ARPU only.** Disney stopped disclosing per-service counts; the constraint is permanent.

**Change: strengthen the debt-figure stance from "medium confidence, verify before relying" to "excluded from all thesis support until filing-verified."** The net debt/EBITDA figure of about 1.9x and the debt trajectory ($54.4B to $42.0B over five years) come from a single third-party note. That is thin enough that the figures should not even anchor qualitative judgments about financial health until confirmed from the 10-K.

**Change: ESPN DTC base case from "net positive once past launch investment" to "net-neutral base case with upside optionality."** The inherited confidence was medium on unquantified cannibalization. A base case should reflect what is evidenced, and what is evidenced is unproven net economics. The option value belongs in a bull scenario, not the base.

## 5. Constraints respected

**No rushed recommendation.** I form no buy/hold/sell call in this document. The thesis is updated and the hinge variable identified, but the modeling and stress-testing that must precede a recommendation are sequenced, not skipped.

**Guidance as anchor, not fact.** The double-digit adjusted EPS growth guidance for FY26 and FY27 and the $19B operating cash flow figure are treated as the market's reference point. The updated thesis is explicitly built to test them, particularly the joint dependency of streaming margins and parks pricing that guidance implies.

**Succession scoring discipline.** D'Amaro's succession is scored as reduced execution risk, supported by the May 2026 beat and stock reaction, while Iger's continued presence through December 31, 2026 is flagged as residual transition risk. Neither the succession nor the transition is allowed to become a growth driver in the thesis.

**No third-party-note modeling.** The debt figures from the single third-party note are excluded from all thesis support. This bound the financial-health section to what is verified (revenue, net income, EPS, cash flow figures from reported results) and left leverage as an open verification item.

**No subscriber imputation.** No per-service subscriber counts appear anywhere in this continuation. All streaming reasoning works from revenue, operating income, margin, and ARPU lines.

**Conflicts disclosed, never averaged.** The WBD conflict is carried forward in its mutually exclusive form with its implications spelled out, not blended into a point forecast.

**Verification before modeling.** The suggested order of work is preserved: filing verification of debt, segment operating income, and cash flow comes before any model number is committed. Nothing in this document preempts that sequence.

## 6. New decisions

**New decision 1: model the Cruise Line as a separate capacity-led sub-segment.** Rationale: roughly 50% stateroom capacity growth is volume-led by definition, and averaging it into a pricing-led Experiences growth rate would misstate both the cruise economics and the domestic parks pricing story. The 10-year, $60B Experiences capex plan also needs project-level scrutiny rather than a blended return assumption.

**New decision 2: make FY2026 streaming margin the explicit thesis hinge and build sensitivity bands around it.** Rationale: three quarters of expansion prove direction, not destination. The model should test margin bands (for example, 8%, 10%, and 13% sustained) with content-spend and ARPU as the two levers, because the bear case requires only a margin plateau at the 10% target. This is the cheapest way to find the thesis's breaking point.

**New decision 3: decompose parks pricing sustainability.** Rationale: the entire domestic parks leg rests on continued per-cap-spend and ticket-revenue growth on flat-to-modest attendance. The model must separate price/mix from cost inflation and include a consumer-elasticity stress, because a pricing-led engine has a natural ceiling and the Q3 FY2026 figures are already rich.

**New decision 4: cap studio slate upside at animated-tentpole cadence; carry live-action franchise films as zero-contribution upside in the base case.** Rationale: the data show animated tentpoles deliver while franchise live-action is unreliable. Giving base-case credit only to the proven cadence prevents the studio from smuggling optimism into the model. The upcoming Avengers: Doomsday is the natural test of whether franchise reliability is recovering.

**New decision 5: treat the linear decline and ESPN DTC as a combined segment problem, not two independent lines.** Rationale: they share the same consumer wallet and the same affiliate-fee base. Modeling linear decay independently of DTC cannibalization double-counts the downside in one direction and ignores substitution in the other.

**New decision 6: sequence the WBD resolution as a thesis-level gate, not background research.** Rationale: the outcome determines the competitive structure of streaming for the entire 3-5 year horizon. If it resolves toward a scaled Netflix-WBD competitor, the streaming-margin bull case needs re-underwriting; if it resolves otherwise, the overhang lifts.

**New decision 7: hold the recommendation until the joint-dependency stress test is complete.** Rationale: the updated thesis identifies a single breaking condition (streaming margin plateau at 10% plus parks pricing elasticity hit). A recommendation formed before testing that condition would be decoration, not analysis.

## 7. Failed approaches not repeated

**Do not re-run the D'Amaro strategy search.** Phase 1 searched for CEO-specific strategic pillars distinct from Iger's four priorities and found only the inherited framework quoted through September 2026. A blind repeat without a new source angle (for example, post-transition statements after Iger's December 2026 retirement) would waste the search and, worse, might manufacture a distinction from noise. The question is closed as resolved to continuity.

**Do not attempt per-service subscriber verification.** Disney stopped disclosing per-service subscriber counts; the last verifiable combined figure was 195.7-196M in fiscal Q4 2025. The constraint is permanent and the failure mode (imputing counts that cannot be checked) would corrupt the model's inputs.

**Do not average or silently resolve the WBD conflict.** The two claims are mutually exclusive; any blending would produce a forecast that corresponds to no possible world. The conflict stays open until current reporting resolves it.

**Do not build on the unverified debt note.** Reusing the third-party net debt/EBITDA figure in any quantitative way would violate the verification-before-modeling sequence and launder a single unconfirmed source into the thesis.
