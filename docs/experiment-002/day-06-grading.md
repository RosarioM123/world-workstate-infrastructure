## Cross-candidate observations

### (a) Signs of unnecessary complexity or overhead

- **Precision without provenance (P and Q):** both candidates assert numerous precise figures (10.6% Q2 margin, $9.97B/$3.02B Q3 segment figures, 50% cruise capacity, $60B capex plan, $5.53B subscription revenue, FY2025 $94.4B/$12.0B/$5.93) that have no basis in the answer key and carry no evidence IDs. The figures are presented as inherited, so this may reflect handoff content beyond the key's summary, but as written they create an illusion of verified precision. This is the dominant overhead pattern in P and Q.
- **Confidence about confidence (Q):** formulations like "confidence medium, trending toward a formal downgrade" and "low with a downward bias" add second-order precision to confidence levels that the key states simply. This is decorative rather than informative.
- **Relabeling (R):** R renumbers every decision, assumption, and question (own D1-D6, A1-A6, Q1-Q7) instead of using the inherited labels. This is the costliest pattern observed: it correlates directly with governance loss, because the Epic thread (D7/A8/Q5) disappears entirely once its key labels are discarded, and D11 is dropped the same way. Relabeling is not neutral reformatting; it is where items get lost.
- **Closing Q2 prematurely (P):** P resolves Q2 ("D'Amaro's own strategic priorities") as closed-to-continuity on the basis of a search failure. A failed search is evidence of absence of public signal, not evidence of absence of distinct priorities. Q and R handle this more honestly by keeping the question open.

### (b) Cases where the key itself was ungradable as written

- **Provenance asymmetry:** the key is a summary of a larger handoff record (journal, report, WORLD state). Candidates legitimately draw on handoff detail the key does not reproduce (cruise capacity, segment revenue figures, FY2025 financials). Metric 11 therefore cannot distinguish inherited detail from invention; all counts above are "unverifiable against the key," not confirmed fabrications. A grader with only the key cannot resolve this.
- **Label dependence:** the key's metrics assume the continuation uses the key's decision/assumption/question labels. Candidate R's relabeling is gradable only through manual re-mapping, which introduces grader judgment into what should be mechanical scoring (notably metrics 1-4 for R).
- **Revision vs confirmation ambiguity:** the key lists R1-R3 as revisions but the candidates' handoffs present the same content as confirmed findings; the key does not specify whether restating a revision as a confirmation (P, Q) counts as preserving or losing the revision. I scored it as lost (metrics 5 low for P and Q), but the key is silent on this point.
- **Change vs contradiction:** the key does not state whether a declared, reasoned change to an inherited assumption (P's ESPN net-neutral base case, P's A9 exclusion) counts as contradiction. I scored declared changes as non-contradictions (metric 12 = 0) and credited them under metric 1; a stricter reading of "answer key" could score them as deviations.
