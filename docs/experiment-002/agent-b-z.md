# Intel 3–5 Year Research Continuation

## 1. Updated investment thesis

Intel's investment question has narrowed to a single mechanism: whether the company can convert demonstrated process execution into a *merchant* foundry business, or whether 18A remains a one-customer node funding a structurally unprofitable factory system. The thesis is now conditional rather than directional. If Intel secures at least one significant external customer committing to 14A (or meaningful 18A external volume) by roughly mid-2027, and the foundry operating margin trajectory keeps improving toward break-even, the fixed-asset leverage finally works in its favor — leading-edge capacity, a government backstop, and a rebuilt product line compounding into durable earnings power through 2030. If instead no external customer materializes and 14A is paused or discontinued per management's own ultimatum, Intel becomes a two-tier company: a recovering but share-losing product business financing foundry losses out of operating cash flow and repeated equity dilution, with the government warrant structure blocking the cleanest exit (a foundry separation).

The update is a demotion of the process-execution leg and a promotion of the commercial-validation leg. The bull case's old binding constraint — "can Intel build a leading node" — is answered yes on current evidence (Panther Lake shipping on 18A, Clearwater Forest on schedule). What remains is entirely "will anyone else buy wafers," and the numbers are stark: ~$293M of external revenue in Q2 2026, about 5% of the foundry segment, with the only named customer under new leadership buying on Intel 4, a mature 2021-era node. The bear case has sharpened correspondingly: it is no longer "Intel can't make transistors" but "Intel built leading-edge capacity with no binding external demand, and the government deal structure makes shrinking or spinning out the foundry nearly impossible." Both cases flow through the same choke point — a 14A customer commitment by roughly mid-2027 — making the thesis unusually binary for a company of this scale. The product business provides a floor but not growth: AMD's record 46.2% server *revenue* share in Q1 2026 against only ~33% unit share shows Intel retaining units while losing the high-value mix, and without a mix recovery or AI-accelerator revenue, the product group alone cannot fund the foundry buildout at planned capex.

## 2. Prior decisions inherited

**Kept: the source hierarchy (SEC filings > Intel press releases > reputable press > analyst commentary > aggregators/blogs).** Phase 1 proved this necessary, not ceremonial — the yield debate, the reported $20B raise, and the forward P/E figure all live exclusively in weak sources, and each would have corrupted downstream reasoning if treated as fact. I tighten it: any continuation claim resting solely on an aggregator carries an explicit credibility flag, and any claim used in the thesis needs at least a press-release or major-press source.

**Kept: the joint-test framework (18A internal yield/cost trajectory AND a significant external 14A customer).** Phase 1's most important correction. Either half alone produces false conviction — the first a false bull case, the second without cost discipline equally false (a customer at uneconomic pricing is not validation). I add only a deadline dimension, since management set one: a material customer commitment by roughly mid-2027, or the 14A program is at risk.

**Kept: the guardrail that only binding, disclosed commercial terms count as foundry validation.** No credit for rumored customers, rumored JVs, or internal revenue. The temptation to soften this — "Fortinet is a start" — is rejected: a mature-node win validates that Intel 4 is a serviceable mature node, not a leading-edge merchant foundry.

**Kept: revenue-share, not unit-share, as the primary server competitive metric.** AMD at a record 46.2% revenue share on ~33% unit share is the clearest quantitative read on Intel's mix erosion, and it is the metric that maps to actual earnings. Unit share (~65% Intel) flatters the incumbent.

**Kept: non-GAAP earnings and cash flow, not GAAP net income, as the operating anchor — with the mark-to-market caveat.** The $12.5B Q2 2026 mark-to-market on escrowed government shares dominates GAAP but is non-cash; the dilution overhang it represents is real economics and stays in the thesis as an ownership-structure cost rather than an operating item.

**Changed: the weight of the government stake.** Phase 1 landed at "support strengthens liquidity but is simultaneously a dilution overhang, a governance constraint, and political dependence." I keep all three costs but reweight: the five-year warrant at $20/share for another 5%, exercisable only if Intel drops below 51% foundry ownership, is not just a governance constraint — it is the single largest structural impediment to the bear-case resolution (separating or shrinking foundry). The deal is asymmetrical: downside liquidity insurance for the enterprise, while dilution, dividend restrictions, and the blocked separation all fall on shareholders. I now treat the 51% condition as a hard boundary on strategic optionality.

**Changed: the default reading of "supply-constrained" demand.** Phase 1 carried both readings (genuine demand vs. underinvestment after capex discipline). I tilt toward the skeptical one: an underinvested company looks supply-constrained when demand merely normalizes, and that story requires no new facts — so the burden of proof sits with the demand reading. A prior, not a conclusion.

**Changed: the NVIDIA framing emphasis.** Phase 1 landed on "both dominant competitor and strategic partner." Kept, but the asymmetry is now explicit: NVIDIA wins in every scenario where AI accelerators matter (as competitor if Intel's own accelerators fail, as partner capturing margin on joint products), while Intel's benefit is bounded to CPU attach and PC relevance. The partnership improves Intel's floor; it does not fix the ceiling — standalone accelerator revenue remains near zero (Gaudi 3 missed its $500M goal, Falcon Shores cancelled as a commercial product, SynapseAI discontinued). Read the partnership as evidence NVIDIA finds Intel's capacity and CPU IP useful — mildly validating for the foundry thesis — not as an AI-growth story.

## 3. Assumptions revisited

**"Restructuring charges decline materially after 2026" — REVISED, confidence: low.** Old assumption: the FY2026 GAAP-to-non-GAAP opex gap ($23.0B vs $16.5B, with $4.3B restructuring/other) normalizes post-2026. Trigger: the 14A ultimatum creates a scenario (node paused/discontinued) generating fresh restructuring charges well beyond 2026. The assumption now splits by scenario: charges decline if 14A proceeds with a customer; spike if 14A is paused. Unconditional confidence is low.

**"Fortinet win has no read-through to 18A/14A" — CONFIRMED, confidence: high.** A mature-node customer validates mature-node operations. If anything, the first named external win under a commercially aggressive CEO landing on a five-year-old node is weak signal on leading-edge salability.

**"Government stake provides too-important-to-fail downside protection" — REVISED, confidence: medium.** It de-risks *liquidity* but not *equity value*: protection flows to the enterprise while dilution, dividend restrictions, and the foundry-ownership warrant fall on shareholders. Medium confidence because the arrangement's political durability is itself uncertain.

**"Foundry margin improvement (minus 71.7% to minus 36.2% YoY) signals real cost progress" — CONFIRMED with qualification, confidence: medium.** The Q2 2026 10-Q MD&A evidence is primary-source and genuine: no repeat of the $797M Q2-2025 manufacturing-asset impairment, no Gaudi inventory charges, $226M lower reserves from 18A early-ramp sell-through. Qualification: the improvement is driven by internal volume — precisely the revenue that doesn't validate the merchant thesis. Cost progress is real; commercial progress is not.

**"AI PC and Xeon demand growth supports the product recovery leg" — CONFIRMED, confidence: medium.** AI PC revenue up 26% sequentially to two-thirds of client mix; DCAI at $6.3B (+59% YoY); Xeon 6 among the fastest ramps. Real numbers — but product revenue growth and product *margin* growth differ, and only the latter funds the foundry given the mix-share problem.

**"TSMC JV is rumor, not base case" — CONFIRMED, confidence: high.** No developments since March 2025, and the August 2025 government structure makes a TSMC-operated JV materially harder to construct. Revisited only if named parties confirm talks post-government-stake.

**"The ~10% 18A yield figure is unknowable and unusable" — CONFIRMED, confidence: high.** Phase 1's abandonment of yield-pinpointing was correct and I extend it: the whole yield discourse (10% vs 65–80% vs "7–8% monthly improvement") is noise for investment purposes. Observable variables only — segment margins, management cost commentary, signed customers.

**"Equity-funded buildout is the default financing mode" — NEW, confidence: high.** Across the government primary shares, the NVIDIA $5B, the unconfirmed SoftBank $2B, and directional evidence of further raises, Intel is funding the foundry with equity even as long-term debt jumped $43.0B→$48.5B in one quarter (flow unverified). Cumulative dilution is the primary shareholder cost of the foundry strategy, and any forward share-count work must aggregate every leg.

**DISCARDED: "Internal foundry-segment revenue growth is progress toward merchant validation."** Phase 1 already corrected this; I formally discard any residual form. Internal revenue is a transfer price, not a market signal.

## 6. New decisions made in this continuation

**Decision 1: Make cumulative dilution an explicit analytical pillar.** Rationale: Phase 1 treated each equity leg (government, NVIDIA, SoftBank, the rumored August raise) as a separate ownership fact; the pattern is the point. The buildout is equity-financed, and per-share economics of any future foundry profit must be measured against the fully diluted count. Rests on high-confidence evidence: 433.3M government primary shares, 214.7M+ NVIDIA shares, 274.6M CHIPS-agreement shares with 240.5M more optional.

**Decision 2: Add an explicit deadline (mid-2027) to the joint test.** Rationale: management set the ultimatum; the program should score against it. Rests on Tan's public gating of 14A on a significant external customer. If no commitment by roughly mid-2027, the base case becomes the downside case. This converts an open-ended question into a dated, falsifiable checkpoint.

**Decision 3: Default to the skeptical reading of "supply-constrained" demand.** Rationale: the capacity-underinvestment story requires no new facts; the demand-surge story does. Future demand color must clear a higher bar (disclosed backlog, binding commitments, not adjectives).

**Decision 4: Treat the government warrant's 51% foundry-ownership condition as a hard strategic boundary.** Rationale: it is the only structural feature blocking the bear-case resolution path. Rests on the disclosed warrant terms (5%, $20 strike, five years). Consequence: any scenario involving foundry separation, majority JV, or sale must price in warrant exercise or be discarded.

**Decision 5: Prioritize Altera deconsolidation verification above all other open items.** Rationale: it is the one open question contaminating existing hard data (external foundry revenue attribution) rather than merely adding new data. Resolve before any further work touches the $293M figure.

**Decision 6: Codify "yield discourse is noise" as a standing rule.** Rationale: Phase 1 reached this through failed verification; I formalize it so no future effort re-litigates anonymous-source yield claims. Observable variables only.
