## 1. Updated thesis

Intel is a state- and partner-backed, dilution-funded turnaround. Operating execution is improving, but the outcome that decides the 3-5 year horizon is still unproven in the record: a significant external customer for leading-edge foundry.

- **Internal execution is real but not commercial proof.** Q2 2026 foundry margin improved from −71.7% to −36.2% on internal volume, and DCAI grew 59% on server demand. However, only about $293M of $5.8B in foundry revenue (~5%) was external, versus $307M of $17.8B (~1.7%) in FY2025. External revenue rose from $174M in Q1 to $293M in Q2, but the composition is unclear, and the only named customer (Fortinet, on Intel 4) is not a 14A signal.
- **AI exposure is indirect.** It runs through Xeon, AI PCs, ASICs, packaging and NVIDIA-linked products, not a merchant GPU roadmap.
- **GAAP results are noise.** Q2 GAAP was $(11.0)B, driven by a $12.5B non-cash escrowed-share mark-to-market, against non-GAAP of +$2.2B.
- **Funding is increasingly equity.** Government (433.3M shares), NVIDIA (214.7M) and SoftBank ($2B, closing unverified) are verified or agreed. The $20B raise is a weak-source claim.
- **Leverage depends on that raise.** Net debt was ~$20.8B at 6/30/2026 on secondary sources. If the raise is real and post-dates 6/30, pro forma net debt would be near zero (about $0.8B before fees). This is illustrative arithmetic and not for base-case use.
- **The competitive gap is in mix, not just units.** AMD leads in server revenue share (46.2%, Q1 2026) despite a smaller unit share (~34.5%, Q2 2026).

Confidence: high on the internal/external distinction, medium on the direction of yield and cost improvement, low on any specific yield level.
