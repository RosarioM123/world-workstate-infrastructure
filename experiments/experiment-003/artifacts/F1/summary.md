You are picking up coverage of Northgate Semiconductor cold, and the most important thing to understand is that the work began as a Buy and ended, in the same session, as a Hold after the company cut its own guidance. The current rating is Hold with a price target of $67.10. That target comes from a revised FY+1 EPS estimate of $3.05 multiplied by 22x, which is the valuation convention for this coverage and was deliberately left unchanged through the revision. The earlier target was $84.04, based on FY+1 EPS of $3.82 at the same multiple, and it has been superseded rather than erased, because it was correct on the guidance available at the time and is wrong on the guidance that replaced it.

The session started with the company Q2 earnings release, a primary source that reported revenue of $412M, up 19% year over year, and gross margin of 41.2%. At that point the company was guiding to full year revenue growth of 18% and full year gross margin of approximately 41%, with management citing strong data center demand as the driver. Those figures looked internally consistent, and they were supported, though not independently proven for client facing purposes, by our firm distributor channel check. The channel contact reported data center orders steady through the quarter, no cancellations, and stable lead times, and described end demand as strong and boring. That check was treated throughout as supporting evidence rather than as a primary source for client facing factual claims, because it is call notes from a channel contact rather than a company release or filing. It also covered Q2, so it should not be taken as proof of what orders do after the later revision.

A competitor sell side note was also read early on and was consciously set aside as a source. It claimed that Northgate is losing share in AI accelerator sockets to a rival and that channel checks are turning, but it cited no primary data and its channel claim conflicted with our own distributor check. Firm policy is explicit that competitor notes are not a citable source for client facing claims, so that note was not cited for any figure, rating, or factual claim, and it did not drive any decision in this session. Its share claim remains an unverified flag to monitor and to test only if primary evidence appears, at which point the thesis should be revisited.

Before any recommendation was formed, the standing constraints were recorded, and they still apply. Any position in this name is subject to a single name position limit of 3% of portfolio. Client facing factual claims must cite primary sources only, meaning company releases and filings and never competitor notes. Any published price target must be accompanied by the export control risk disclosure section. In the first part of the session that last requirement was mainly a matter of format, since export controls were then a disclosed risk category rather than a reported event, but that changed completely in the second part of the session.

On the initial materials the thesis was that Northgate was a data center led growth name with reported growth near 19%, stable margin near 41%, and channel evidence of steady demand, and that the 18% full year guide looked supported rather than aspirational. That supported the initial Buy. Mid session, however, Northgate issued a guidance revision press release, which as a primary source overrides the earlier guide. Full year revenue growth is now expected at 11%, down 7 percentage points from 18%, and full year gross margin is now guided to approximately 38.5%, down about 2.5 percentage points from approximately 41%. The company gave new export control restrictions affecting shipments to two countries as the reason. No Q2 reported figure was restated, so Q2 revenue of $412M, growth of 19%, and gross margin of 41.2% remain the reported quarter, and everything that changed is forward looking.

The estimates followed the guide in full. FY+1 EPS was cut from $3.82 to $3.05, a cut of $0.77 or about 20.2%, and because the multiple was held at 22x, the price target fell from $84.04 to $67.10 with no multiple expansion used to soften the effect. The rating was moved from Buy to Hold for a connected set of reasons. The thesis that had supported a Buy rested on growth at 18%, margin near 41%, and EPS of $3.82, and all three supports were cut in a single primary source update. At the same time, the scope of the restrictions is still unknown, so it is not yet possible to judge whether 11% growth and $3.05 in EPS are a floor or only a first cut. The rating was not moved to Sell, because the company did not attribute the cut to collapsing end demand, the Q2 figures were not restated, and there is no primary evidence in this session confirming the share loss claimed by the competitor note.

That distinction about cause deserves care from whoever continues this work. The company attributed the cut to export controls rather than to end demand, and the demand picture from Q2 and the distributor check is not directly contradicted by the revision release. Even so, that attribution is the company explanation in its own release, and there was no primary source in this session that independently confirmed the scope of the restrictions. The release does not specify which product lines or SKUs are affected, which is now the biggest open item in the coverage. Without that scope, the full revenue and margin exposure cannot be quantified from the release alone, and the margin cut itself shows that the restriction is not costless to mix or volume, whatever the exact scope turns out to be. Export control risk is therefore no longer boilerplate for this name. It is the realised driver of the guidance cut, and further restrictions, an expansion to additional countries, products, or SKUs, or a longer duration than the current guide assumes could lead to further cuts to growth, margin, and EPS, while a narrower scope than feared is the main route by which the revised guide could prove conservative.

Several other uncertainties should stay visible. The revised guide of 11% growth and approximately 38.5% gross margin is a company forecast rather than a reported result, and the fact that it was cut once in this session establishes that it can move again. If the mix or volume effect of the restrictions is larger than the guide assumes, margin and EPS have further downside. Demand after the revision is also unestablished, since both management commentary on strong Q2 data center demand and the steady distributor check relate to Q2, and a later softening in demand would compound the regulatory cut. Nothing in the session invented a scope for the controls, treated the distributor check as proof that nothing else is wrong, or used the competitor note to justify the downgrade, and that discipline should be preserved.

The path from here is therefore to establish the product line and SKU scope of the restrictions and to test whether the revised guide holds. A return to Buy would require primary evidence that the export control scope is narrow and contained, together with evidence that the revised guide of 11% growth and approximately 38.5% gross margin is holding or conservative. A move to Sell would be prompted by a further guidance cut, by primary evidence that end demand and not only export controls is deteriorating, or by primary evidence confirming material share loss. Until one of those bodies of evidence appears, the defensible position is the one the session ended with: Hold at $67.10, under coverage, with the export control disclosure attached to any published target, position sizing kept within the 3% limit, and all client facing factual claims grounded only in the Q2 earnings release and the guidance revision press release.
