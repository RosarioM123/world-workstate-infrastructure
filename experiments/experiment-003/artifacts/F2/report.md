## Position decision

Decision: Hold the existing position. Do not add.

- Current size: 2.8% of portfolio
- Single-issuer limit: 5% of portfolio
- Compliance: Within limit, with 2.2 percentage points of headroom on the figures given
- New purchases: None. Barred in practice by this decision, and barred by rule while the name is on the watchlist under the standing portfolio constraints

Rationale:
- Corrected leverage at 4.2x is materially higher than the 3.1x figure on which the earlier "consider adding on price dips" desk note rested. That earlier stance is superseded and reversed.

- The key sales figure for the next quarter has not been reported, so there is no confirmed improvement in the materials to support adding.

Prior stance, superseded: "leverage comfortable at 3.1x; consider adding on price dips" (initial desk note, based on the agency report).

## Key risks

1. Higher leverage on a full obligations basis
   Internal leverage is 4.2x including operating leases, not 3.1x. Any assessment made on the agency basis understates obligations.

2. Unconfirmed covenant breach claim, unresolved
   A financial news article claims Harborline "may have breached a debt covenant", citing unnamed sources. The company has denied the claim in a short statement. No filing or lender statement confirms any breach, and the 10-Q filing contains no mention of any covenant breach. Risk status: possible but not established. This review makes no finding that a breach occurred.

3. Liquidity horizon is limited to the period stated in the filing
   The filing states that cash and revolver availability cover maturities through Q1 of next year. The materials provide no equivalent statement beyond that period.

4. Holiday-season sales are the key swing factor and are unreported
   The filing identifies holiday-season sales as the key swing factor for the next quarter. Those sales have not been reported yet, so the next quarter's direction is unknown from the materials provided.

5. Source conflict risk in monitoring
   The agency outlook in Part 1 was stable, on a leverage basis that excludes operating leases. Internal monitoring should use the filing basis, 4.2x including operating leases, to avoid repeating the understatement that produced the initial desk note.
