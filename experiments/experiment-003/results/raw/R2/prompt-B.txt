You are resuming planning of an onboarding user study that a previous
research agent started. Using only the handoff material provided, produce
a Continuation Document with exactly these sections:

1. **Where things stand:** the current study format, session length used

   status.
2. **Decisions in force:** every decision that currently governs the
   study, with the reason it was made. Separately list any decisions or
   instructions that are no longer in force.
3. **Assumptions and facts:** the assumptions the plan rests on, labeled

   corrected. Keep facts distinct from assumptions.

   Flag it; do not invent answers.
7. **Next actions:** the concrete next steps, in order. Do not propose
   anything the constraints prohibit.
8. **Final run-of-show:** a short run-of-show and recruitment message (at
   most about 300 words) using only the currently authoritative format,

# Handoff Summary: Onboarding User Study Plan

You are picking up the planning for a moderated user study of a new onboarding flow, and the plan you should work from is the revised one described here, not the version it started as. The study aims for 12 participants and will now run as remote moderated sessions, with each session planned at 25 minutes and each participant paid an incentive of $30. Recruitment is still underway, because only 3 of the 12 participants have confirmed so far, which leaves 9 places unfilled.

The format changed during planning. The plan as first drafted called for in-person moderated sessions in the usability lab, but a recruitment update then showed that recruitment was falling short, with only 3 of 12 participants confirmed, and that lab scheduling was the main friction cited by recruits. On that evidence, the research lead decided to switch the study to remote moderated sessions, which removes the lab scheduling friction. The switch also resolved an ethics constraint that had been noted from the start, since the existing IRB approval covers remote moderated sessions only and in-person sessions would have needed an amendment. The current remote format sits within the existing approval, so no amendment is needed for it, whereas returning to in-person sessions would bring that requirement back. The original in-person version remains part of the record as superseded rather than deleted, so the change and the reason for it stay visible.

The session length changed for a different reason. The original figure of 45 minutes per session was a planning estimate made before any pilot run, not a measured result. A pilot run of the session script then took 25 minutes, and because a measured pilot is stronger evidence than the initial pre-pilot estimate, planning should now use 25 minutes per session. The 45 minute figure survives only as the corrected initial estimate, and its underlying assumption has been invalidated by the pilot evidence.

The participant incentive changed as well, and here two instructions conflicted. The earlier figure was $50 per participant, which came from the original project brief written last month and was the figure in force when drafting began. An updated budget memo from the research lead later set the incentive at $30 per participant, in order to keep the 12 participant study within the total study budget cap of $900, a standing constraint owned by the research lead. Where two instructions conflict, the later one from the instruction owner governs, and that rule settles this case in favour of the memo: the research lead owns the budget, and the memo is the later instruction, so $30 is authoritative and $50 is superseded, kept visible only with its provenance and the reason it was replaced.

What remains open is recruitment. The study is still short of its target, and recruitment for the remaining places has not been completed. Beyond what is stated here, the materials give no additional session dates, recruitment channels, or participant details, so none of that is known yet. Your next step is therefore to continue from this revised plan, remote sessions of 25 minutes at $30 per participant within the $900 cap and the existing remote-only IRB approval, and to carry recruitment forward until the remaining places are filled.
