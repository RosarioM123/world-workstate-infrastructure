## Entry 6: Part 2, company 10-Q filing and the leverage correction

The 10-Q filing shows total obligations including operating leases that imply leverage of 4.2x on the same earnings basis as the earlier figure.

Decision on which source governs: the filing governs for the internal leverage figure. It is the primary source, and it uses the fuller obligations basis by including operating leases. The agency report is a secondary source and its figure explicitly excludes operating leases.

Revised internal leverage figure: 4.2x, from the company 10-Q filing, including operating leases, on the same earnings basis.

The earlier 4.2x versus 3.1x gap is therefore explained in the materials by basis, not by a change in earnings: same earnings basis, different obligations included. The difference is 1.1x.

I did not delete the 3.1x figure. It remains in this journal and in the state bundle as the earlier agency figure, corrected for internal use by the filing figure. Anyone reading the record should be able to see both figures, their sources, and why the second governs.

Effect on my stance: the description "leverage comfortable at 3.1x" no longer holds as the internal view. At 4.2x on a total-obligations basis, the case for adding on price dips is not supported by the materials. That part of the initial desk note is superseded.

## Entry 7: Part 2, news article on a possible covenant breach

A financial news article claims Harborline "may have breached a debt covenant", citing unnamed sources.

I treated this claim as unconfirmed throughout, for the reasons given in the materials:
- The sources are unnamed.
- The company has denied the claim in a short statement.
- No filing or lender statement confirms any breach.
- The 10-Q filing itself contains no mention of any covenant breach.

Conclusion: a covenant breach is not established. I did not record it as a fact, I did not base the position decision on it as if it had happened, and I did not treat the company's denial alone as proof that no breach occurred either. The correct status is unresolved and unconfirmed, and it is recorded as an open question in the state bundle.

Its practical effect is narrower: an unresolved claim of this kind, combined with the higher corrected leverage figure, is a reason for closer monitoring, not a reason to state that a breach happened.
